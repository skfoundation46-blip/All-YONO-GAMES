How Indian Rummy is normally played

Most online Indian Rummy tables use the familiar 13-card format. Each player is dealt 13 cards from two decks. On your turn you draw one card (from the closed deck or the open discard) and then discard one card, keeping your hand at 13.

The goal is to arrange every card into valid groups and declare before the other players. Two rules are essential:

  • You need at least two sequences (runs of three or more consecutive cards of the same suit).
  • At least one of those sequences must be pure — no joker used as a substitute.

A pure sequence example is 5♥ 6♥ 7♥. An impure sequence can use a joker to fill a gap. Sets are three or four cards of the same rank in different suits. Face cards and aces usually count as 10 points; numbered cards count as their face value; jokers count as zero when used correctly. The winner of a hand scores zero. Losers are charged the points of their ungrouped cards, normally capped at 80. A wrong declaration (missing the pure sequence) often carries the full 80-point penalty.

Common table formats are Points (single hand settled by point value), Pool (101 or 201, players drop out when they cross the limit) and Deals (fixed number of hands played with chips). Exact table rules, joker behaviour and drop penalties are shown inside the app and can differ slightly between operators.
