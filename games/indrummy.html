How classic 13-card rummy is played

Each player receives 13 cards from two standard decks plus jokers. The goal is to form valid melds: pure sequences (consecutive cards of the same suit without a joker), impure sequences, and sets (three or four cards of the same rank). At least one pure sequence is required to declare a valid hand.

Points Rummy finishes in one deal; the winner takes the point value of the opponents’ unmatched cards. Pool Rummy (commonly 101 or 201) continues until players are eliminated by reaching the point limit. Deals Rummy is played over a fixed number of hands, with the highest chip total winning. Unmatched face cards count as 10 points; number cards count at face value; jokers are zero. A wrong declaration typically costs the maximum 80 points.
